# Delivery routes and refusal recovery

Read this reference when a computer result needs delivery diagnosis, refusal recovery or disturbance handling. Routine successful actions do not require it. The main skill's access, current-evidence, postcondition and uncertain-effect rules still apply; this reference explains delivery and recovery without granting another route or permission.

## Background work and the foreground fallback

Work runs in the background so the person can keep working. For each step the host picks a route that stays out of the person's way: the accessibility interface where the app supports it, or input routed only to the target's window where this version of Atd offers that. Such a route never moves their pointer, takes their keyboard focus, activates, raises or minimizes a window, switches Spaces or touches the clipboard. You choose steps, never routes or how they are delivered. The person can follow along in the native monitor.

In an interactive task, when no background route can do a left `click`, a `key`, a `type` or a `drag` step on an app, including a route the host switched off after it disturbed the person, the host completes that step in the foreground within the same `act`. It waits for a pause in the person's typing and clicking, briefly brings the app and its window to the front, sends that step's clicks and keys straight to the app, then puts back the app and window they had; the person's pointer never moves. The result's `delivery` is then `foreground`, and its evidence says what was brought forward and put back. This needs no request and no approval beyond the action's usual one, and you cannot ask for it: no field selects it, so never ask the person to hand over the screen, pointer or keyboard, and never use shell scripts, AppleScript or other computer-control tools to get past a limit. The foreground does not take steps that need the real pointer (hover-only controls, drags with an end outside the step's window, apps that follow only the real cursor), areas an app draws without accessibility elements, or keys and typing in Chromium, Electron and CEF apps, which take no keys Atd posts to them, in front or not; those go to the person. Websites, capture-point steps and runs that follow a recipe (automation runs and their trials) stay in the background, and a foreground step never restores a minimized window, switches Spaces or leaves full screen.

Opening a target never brings it forward: an app stays where it is, and a website opens in a background tab without bringing its browser to the front. Should `requestAccess` still report `access.activated` (opening made an app active anyway), tell the person. An app that was not running starts in the background and can take a few seconds to show its window; when `requestAccess` returns no target yet, call it again instead of starting the app yourself. Never open, activate or switch to a target with a shell command or AppleScript: that brings it in front of the person while they work.

When nothing the task may use can do a step, the host refuses it, and the answer says what to do next (an action result's `next`, or the refusal's own text). Read its `error.code` and `error.reason`:

- `noBackgroundRoute`: no background route reaches the step, and the host could not complete it in the foreground either: the task stays in the background, the limit is one the foreground does not lift, or bringing the app forward failed. Do not retry it or look for a way around it. Unless the reason below says otherwise, ask the person to do that one step themselves, saying exactly what it is, wait until they say it is done, then observe again and continue from the new state. The `reason` names the limit:
  - `dragAndDrop`: a native drag and drop that needs the real pointer, such as between windows or apps, or a drag with an end outside the step's window; nothing was sent. A drag with both ends on one window works where the host offers a route for it, or in an interactive task in the foreground, unless capture points locate it.
  - `hover`: hovering, hover menus and tooltips, which need the real pointer.
  - `opaqueSurface`: an area the app draws without accessibility elements, such as a canvas, video or game; input Atd posts there misses in the foreground too.
  - `pointerToolkit`: an app that follows only the real pointer, so input sent to its window would miss, in the foreground too.
  - `secureInput`: secure text entry such as a password field; the person types it, and you never ask them for the secret.
  - `menuUnavailable`, `keyUnavailable`, `textUnavailable`: no route this task may use for that menu item, that key or shortcut, or that text on this target. A Chromium, Electron or CEF app takes no keys Atd posts to it, in front or not, so keys and text there go to the person. When the message names an input method, which would turn the keys into text of its own, ask the person to switch to a keyboard layout such as ABC so Atd can type, or to type it themselves, and send the step again only once they say they switched.
  - `minimized`, `offSpace`, `fullScreen`: the window is minimized, or it is on another Space, on one the host cannot tell, or in a full-screen Space; the host never restores, moves or switches to it, not even for a foreground step. Steps through the accessibility interface still work on a window on another Space or in full screen. Ask the person to restore the window, bring it onto the desktop they are using or leave full screen, then observe again.
  - `covered`: the host brought the app forward, but macOS mouse-down hit-testing did not confirm the intended target window at the input point. Another window, including Atd’s own panel, may intercept it, or the hit could not be resolved. The host sends nothing and restores the previous foreground. It uses actual per-point input hit-testing rather than window rectangles; transparent or mouse-ignoring overlays follow the system’s hit-test behavior. Ask the person to move that window aside or to do the step themselves; once they say so, observe again, and send the step in a new act only if they moved the window rather than doing the step.
  - `notActivated`: macOS did not let the host bring the app and its window to the front for a foreground step, so it sent nothing and put back what it had changed.
  - `openBrowser`: the site's browser environment could not be started in the background, so the person opens it. Access stays approved; ask them to open it with the Open browser button the conversation shows, or from Settings → Computer use, then call `requestAccess` again.
  - `screenLocked`: the Mac is locked, and macOS hides app windows and drops input to them until it is unlocked. Do not ask the person to do the step; tell them work in this app continues once they unlock the Mac, keep working on browser targets meanwhile if the task allows, and observe again when they are back.
  - `noRoute`: no other limit applies, but no route this task may use reaches this step on this target.
- `targetInUse`: the person is working in the target right now (it is in front of them and they typed, clicked or scrolled in the last few seconds), so the host yielded and sent nothing. A target in front of a person who is only watching is not in use, and actions on it run as usual. Never ask them to stop. Wait a few seconds with `observe.afterMs` and try again; if it stays in use, tell them you will continue once they are done with it, and stop for now.
- `inputInUse`: the person is typing, clicking or scrolling right now, in any app, so the host held the step back rather than send input while they do, or stopped a step it was doing in the foreground as soon as they started and put back what they had in front of them; with `effect` `none`, nothing was sent. This passes in a moment and is not a missing route: never ask them to stop, and never ask them to do the step themselves. Wait a few seconds with `observe.afterMs`, then try the step again as a new act; if it keeps happening, tell them you will continue once they pause, and stop for now. When `effect` is not `none`, part of the step went out before they started: read `effect` and `retry` as for any uncertain result.
- `needsConsent`: screen capture was never prepared for Atd, and a run never asks for it while working. Continue without screenshots when the accessibility tree is enough; otherwise tell the person to prepare screen access once in Settings → Computer use.
- `disturbed`: the action changed something of the person's; `disturbances` lists each change (`kind`), who caused it as far as the host can tell (`attribution`) and whether it was `repaired`. The host repaired what it could and ended the action. On an app, a background route that caused it stays off until the person replies, and until Atd restarts once that route has disturbed them twice; a `click`, `key`, `type` or `drag` that needs a route that is off runs in the foreground in an interactive task, and any other such step is refused with `noBackgroundRoute`. A step the host did in the foreground switches no route off; it ends `disturbed` when the host could not put back what it changed. Stop acting on this target, tell the person what was disturbed and anything not repaired, and continue only when they ask you to. Read `effect` and `retry` as for any uncertain result.
- `needsPermission`: a macOS privacy permission is missing; tell the person which one the message names instead of retrying.

A result can also list `disturbances` without ending in `disturbed`. One attributed to the person or to nobody in particular (`unknown`), such as keys they pressed while the target was in front, their switching apps or a window raised after they did, is only reported: mention any that matter to the task and go on. In an interactive task this also includes a window a background route raised over the person's or an app it made active, which the host repaired by putting back the app and window they had: the action was not stopped, the host switched that route off for this kind of step in this app, and later steps that need it run in the foreground. Continue the task as `next` says, without stopping or asking the person. A step the person did themselves is their action, not one you verified: observe and confirm the state it left before relying on it.
